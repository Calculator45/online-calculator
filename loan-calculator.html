How loan payments are calculated

A standard loan payment depends on the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the length of the loan. Payments are usually made monthly.

Monthly payment formula M = P × [r(1 + r)ⁿ] ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]

P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the total number of monthly payments.

Loan Payment Example

Suppose you borrow $10,000 at an annual interest rate of 6% for 3 years. Using monthly repayments, the loan would require approximately 36 monthly payments of $304.22.

Over the full loan term, you would repay approximately $10,952, meaning about $952 would be paid in interest.

What affects your loan payment?

Your monthly loan payment depends mainly on the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the repayment period. A higher interest rate generally increases both the monthly payment and the total interest paid.

Extending the loan term can reduce the monthly payment, but it may increase the total amount of interest paid over the life of the loan. Actual loans may also include fees or other charges that are not included in this calculator.
